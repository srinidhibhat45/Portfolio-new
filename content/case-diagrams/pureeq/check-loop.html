Inside the listening check

Each beep is chosen to teach the most about your ears

The check keeps one running best guess for all 7 pitches in both ears, with how unsure it is about each. Every answer narrows it.

1Best guess so far

For each pitch and ear: the quietest beep you can likely hear, and how sure the check is. Neighbouring pitches and the two ears inform each other.

→

2Pick the next beep

Pitch: 1, 2, 4, 8 kHz and 500 Hz first, then 3 and 6 kHz, ears taking turns. Level: the one expected to shrink the doubt most, never more than 10 dB above anything played so far.

→

3Play it

Two pulsed beeps, 225 ms on and 200 ms off, with soft edges so nothing clicks. Each wavers ±5% in pitch, so one narrow earbud peak cannot pass for a weak band.

↓

5Update the guess

The answer moves the estimate for that pitch and nudges its neighbours and the other ear. Every answer is also saved with the profile.

←

4Answer

Yes, No, or play it again once. Every 8 to 12 turns a silent turn plays nothing, to measure how often you guess Yes. A replayed beep is scored as two chances to hear it, so replaying cannot make a band look better than it is.

Stop

A pitch is finished in one ear once the doubt falls to 3.5 dB (Standard), after at least two beeps and one Yes. Quick stops at 4.5 dB and Thorough at 2.75 dB.

dB (decibels) measures loudness. Values are the app's settings in version 1.7.3.
