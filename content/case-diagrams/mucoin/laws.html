Three rules of thumb

The design rule behind each step

Hick's Law

The more choices on a screen, the longer it takes to decide.

In MuCoinThe income step asks for two things only: the amount and the source.
Miller's Law

People can hold about seven things in mind at once, give or take two.

In MuCoinExpenses go in one at a time. A summary holds the running picture, so memory does not have to.
Jakob's Law

People expect a new screen to work like the ones they already know.

In MuCoinVariable expenses reuse the fixed-expense pattern. The goal form and the edit forms look like the ones before them.
