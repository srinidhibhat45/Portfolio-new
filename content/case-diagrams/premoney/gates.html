Inside the parser

Five checks, always in this order

A message must pass every check to become an entry. The order is part of the design, and tests hold it in place.

1

Never a payment

OTPs come first, because they often quote the amount. Then failed payments and statements.

Rejected: OTP, failed, statement. No amount of proof overrides these.

2

Advert or request?

Promotional wording, future dates and payment requests.

Rejected: promotional, future or requested. Skipped when the bank's own marks are overwhelming.

3

Which way did money go?

Card names are blanked first. Then the first verb wins, so "debited from A, credited to B" is a payment out.

Rejected: nothing says money moved.

4

How much?

Balances, limits and offer figures are set aside by the words around them. The earliest number left wins.

Rejected: no amount, or over ₹5 crore.

5

Enough proof?

Count the bank's marks and the advert's tells. The more it reads like an advert, the more proof it needs.

Rejected: advert, or not enough proof.

Accepted

Now the fields are read: merchant, account digits, reference, channel, date. Each one it cannot read lowers the confidence.

Why checks 1 and 2 come before direction: so the message tester reports an advert as an advert, not as whichever later check it happened to fail.
